# Phase I Rule Decisions and Source Ambiguities

**Build:** `GAME-NIGHT-STAGING-PHASE-I-THREE-GAMES-07`

## Mexican Train

The supplied rules are implemented as a three-round match in which the lowest cumulative remaining-pip total wins.

The initial first player is selected through the supplied temporary high-pip draw. Those temporary tiles are returned before the actual deal.

The engine is established by the highest double held by the eligible current player. If no double is held, that player draws once where possible and passes the search onward.

A private train becomes open after a failed draw/play sequence. The saved family avatar is used as the visible open-train marker in the tabletop presentation.

A double creates a forced closure obligation on that train. The digital game automatically announces when a player has one tile remaining rather than requiring a separate announcement button.

Regional Mexican Train variants differ. This module intentionally follows the supplied three-round, opening, open-train and forced-double structure rather than silently importing another online variation.

## Skip-Bo

The implemented deck has 162 cards: 12 copies of each number 1 through 12 and 18 Wild cards.

The host can choose Stock sizes of 10, 20 or 30. A 30-card Stock is limited to 2 to 4 players because five or six 30-card Stocks would require 150 or 180 cards before five-card hands and a Draw Pile are created. The 20-card option supports five or six players.

The base implementation is standard individual play. Partnership mode and optional long-form scoring remain future options, as allowed by the supplied specification.

A player draws to five at turn start. Emptying the hand during the turn immediately draws five more and continues the same turn. Discarding one hand card to one of four Discard Piles ends the turn. Clearing the Stock Pile wins immediately.

## Backgammon

The first staging implementation is a standard single game. Its score architecture can later support match play.

The move engine evaluates complete legal sequences so it can enforce maximum dice usage, the larger-die rule, doubles, Bar priority and legal intermediate positions.

Optional Automatic Doubles, Beavers and Jacoby rules are implemented as host settings and default to off.

Dice results are generated by the authoritative room using cryptographic random integers. The dimensional dice presentation animates the predetermined result. It is not a rigid-body WebGL physics simulation.

The game supports normal wins, gammons and backgammons, multiplied by the doubling-cube value.
